# Page 01 — Opening

**Label:** Personal project

**Title:** A notebook that travels better

**Body:**
I’ve worked with leather for over a decade. What began as a hobby gradually became an interest in how materials, construction, and small details shape the experience of using an object.

# Page 02 — The problem

**Label:** What was wrong

Intro:
While travelling, I usually carry a notebook alongside my passport 
and documents.

For a period I used an A6 Filofax, but the ring mechanism interrupted 
the writing experience and prevented the notebook from lying flat.

I returned to the traveller’s notebook format for its flexibility 
and portability, though over time several aspects of the object 
started to feel unresolved in use.


Problem 1 — The leather collects dust and fibres
The rough flesh side faces inward, so it collects dust and paper fibres with regular use. Over time, it starts to look dirty rather than naturally worn. The texture is intended to feel raw and tactile, but I found it difficult to maintain in daily travel.
Problem 2 — The cover is thicker than it needs to be
At around 2mm thick, it adds weight and bulk without contributing much additional structure. For something carried daily through airports and in pockets, I wanted something lighter and easier to handle.
Problem 3 — The lead seal changes quickly with use
The lead seal oxidises and deforms relatively quickly with use, which changes the appearance of the notebook sooner than I would want.
Problem 4 — The colour palette reinforced the wrong qualities
The available colours leaned heavily toward dark browns and black. On a material that already picks up dust, fibres, and visible wear easily, the overall effect felt heavier and more worn than I wanted.






# Page 03 — What I changed and why

**Label:** The decisions

**Intro:**  
Each decision came from extended daily use — reducing bulk, improving durability, and making the notebook feel cleaner and more considered over time.

---

### Row 1 — Leather

~~Crazy Horse Leather, 2mm~~ — heavy, rough-surfaced, collects wear quickly

Smooth calfskin, 1.4mm. Commonly used in structured leather goods — lighter, cleaner to handle, and more refined in the way it ages.

---

### Row 2 — Dyeing

~~Surface-dyed or undyed~~

Aniline-dyed through the entire hide. Scratches, edges, and worn areas retain the same teal rather than exposing a lighter base underneath.

---

### Row 3 — Flesh side and edges

~~Raw, untreated~~

Burnished throughout with Seiwa colourless gum — including the flesh side, edges, and elastic holes. This seals the fibres, reduces fraying, and gives the notebook a cleaner finish in daily use.

---

### Row 4 — Hardware

~~Lead closure seal~~

Solid brass hardware. Chosen for the way it wears over time, and for the warmer contrast it creates against the teal leather.

---

### Row 5 — Closure

~~Standard cord wrap~~

A colour-matched elastic closure designed to visually recede into the object. Anchored through a burnished punched hole with a solid brass stud.
